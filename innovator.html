Mark Dean Biography

Early Life

Mark Dean was an engineer and inventor that helped develop some of the most important early personal computer technology. He was born on March 2, 1957, in Jefferson's city in Tennessee, United States. Mark dean earned a bachelor’s degree in electrical engineering from the university of Tennessee and would later receive a master's degree and PHD in electrical engineering from Stanford University.

Career and Achievements

Dean also has made many contributions to the field of technology. He was indicted into the Nation Inventors Hall of Fame, was named an IBM fellow in 1995, and received “Black Engineer of the Year President’s Award”. He has many notable inventions including the personal computer, the first colored PC monitor, led the team that developed the first Gigahertz chip (which allows for one billion instruction sub-steps to be done in one second), led the team that developed the first super-computer. The most impressive of all, he holds 3 of the original 9 patents for IBM, which is a major pioneering company in the IT industry. Currently he holds 40+ patents for IBM. Mark Dean is a pioneer in the computation thinking pillar of informatics, as he led the teams of many critical inventions that we use in our everyday life, such as the gigahertz chip. Typically, computers nowadays have chips that are around 3.35hz-5hz, but Mark Dean laid the groundwork for these chips to be built on with his invention of the gigahertz chip. He also invented a microcomputer system with bus controls. This invention allows for a peripheral of the computer (GPU, DMA) to take control of the data control, allowing the CPU to have less workload. This allows for faster data transfer as it doesn’t have to go through the CPU every time to process data. This invention is still used today, with the basic idea of bus controls being used in every PC in the current technological era.

Impact on Technology

Mark Deans contributions to computers were early on into the development of the computer. While working at IBM he founded many things to go along with what we see in computers today. However, his biggest success is creating the ISA Bus which in simple terms basically added hardware into the computer. These would be things like sound cards, graphic cards, and finally memory. The idea of being able to add stuff to computers to make them better but without changing a lot about the outside appearance of the computer has shaped how we build and think about modern computers.

Legacy

Mark Dean's legacy ultimately lays with being a pioneer in the technology, while being a black man in a white-dominated field. He made 3/9 original IBM patents and his the ideas of his innovaitons are still being used today. His hard work and determination through his struggles have helped inspired countless others in the STEM field to pursue their dreams regardless of what challenges they may face on the way there.
